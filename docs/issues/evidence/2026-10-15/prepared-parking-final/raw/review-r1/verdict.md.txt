**Required finding R1 — retain an unobserved rewarm failure when closure interrupts recovery**

Requirement: TT-008 acceptance criteria 4 and 7, including TT-006’s inherited requirements to track lifecycle work and avoid reporting callback failures as successful cleanup.

Location: `src/TurboTest.EFCore/Internal/TtSlotPool.cs`, `RewarmAsync` lines 229–240, `RecoverFailedOperationAsync` lines 454–468, and `ReplenishAfterDeletion` lines 479–487.

`RewarmAsync` computes `reportAtDrain` once, before waiting for the cleanup permit and physical deletion. That value becomes stale across those awaits.

A deterministic ordering exposes the defect:

1. Rewarm verification throws a material error while the pool is live. `reportAtDrain` becomes `false`.
2. Recovery enters `_delete` and waits for confirmation.
3. Factory closure calls `StopAcceptingWork`, or pool disposal sets `_closed`. Pending acquisition is rejected.
4. Deletion completes. `ReplenishAfterDeletion` removes the reservation, while `ReservePending` correctly refuses replacement because closure has started.
5. The original material failure is discarded because `reportAtDrain` remains `false`. Drain finds no retained error and can complete successfully.

No verified replacement restored service, and no caller received the material error. This is therefore different from the intended transparent, successful live recovery. The cleanup-retry path also needs consideration: it carries the failure while retrying, but stopping/cancellation can end that episode without transferring the unobserved failure to drain.

Existing `SlotRewarmDrainTests`, `FactoryRewarmDrainTests`, and native closure tests begin closure before releasing verification. They protect the opposite ordering and do not detect this case.

Required outcome: retain the error obligation through the recovery episode until recovery succeeds or the material diagnostic is delivered. Closure decisions and diagnostic transfer must account for closure during cleanup/retry, rather than relying on the earlier Boolean snapshot. Preserve transparent successful live recovery, cooperative cancellation, bounded retries, single deletion ownership, and delivery once.

Add deterministic component RED evidence that holds recovery deletion after a live verification failure, starts closure, then releases deletion. Assert the material diagnostic, no replacement, confirmed resource release, and successful subsequent cleanup without repeated delivery. Calibrate the supported ordering through the prepared fixture boundary. This finding is based on the actual control flow; I did not execute a reproduction under the read-only review restriction.

**Test effectiveness, architecture and alternatives**

The tests generally assert observable behavior: independent PostgreSQL identity/session/data queries, finite allocation/deletion guards, held asynchronous boundaries, and literal expected seed/sequence values. Component tests provide fast rule feedback; native tests calibrate real external boundaries. Corrected REDs distinguish missing behavior from setup failures.

Coverage demonstrates substantial execution, not complete assertion protection. The current full report executes 496/521 instrumented lines in `TtSlotPool`, 358/361 in `TtPreparedPool`, 214/221 in `TtFactory`, and 33/33 in `TtPreparedConfiguration`. R1 illustrates why executed branches do not establish all relevant asynchronous orderings. Full mutation remains correctly pending the final immutable committed coding-session snapshot.

One ownership ledger, existing preparation/cleanup permits, shared verification and explicit maintenance are proportionate choices. A separate parking controller or timer would add unnecessary ownership complexity. Releasing preparation before recovery cleanup avoids the tested permit inversion; separating admission closure from physical teardown protects held contexts.

For R1, the clearer alternative is to represent the pending diagnostic as part of the owned recovery episode and settle it with recovery, rather than infer its disposition from a pre-await closure snapshot. The repair should remain within the existing owner.

**Reusable lesson candidate**

Owner: the project’s lifecycle review/test practice.

Evidence: R1 remains possible despite covered rewarm failure branches and multiple passing closure tests.

Proposed guidance: when shutdown can interrupt recovery, test closure before failure, during cleanup, and during retry; retain material diagnostics until the episode’s outcome is known.

Downside: additional interleaving tests can become costly and brittle, so use deterministic component gates and limited native calibration. Disposition: proposed in this review only; no standing guidance or memory was changed.
